Weight from length

Guess first. A shark twice as long as another: is it twice as heavy, four times, or eight times?

length-weight law
weight grows like a power of length
exponent
the power, close to 3 for most fish

A shark twice as long weighs about eight times as much, because weight grows like length cubed.

Error: 0Exponent: 3.0

The curve fits best when the exponent is close to 3.

Move the exponent and watch the error. The worked equation below follows the shark you hover.

…weight, kg=0.004a, a constant×…length, cmb

\[ W = a\,L^{b} \]

  • \(W\) is the weight and \(L\) the length.
  • \(b\) is the exponent: about 3, since a shark grows in length, height and width together.

Common belief. Twice as long means twice as heavy.

Why it fails. The body also grows taller and wider.

Better account. Weight grows like length cubed.

Debug tip. If a fit looks wrong, plot the leftovers (residuals). A pattern in them means the model is missing something.

import numpy as np
L = np.array([40, 60, 80])
W = 0.004 * L ** 3 / 1000   # grams to kg
print(W)
[0.256 0.864 2.048]

Quick quiz

A shark three times as long weighs about how many times as much?

About 27 times, since three cubed is 27.

Exercises

Theory

  1. theory Show that if \(W = aL^b\), then \(\log W\) is a straight line in \(\log L\). What are its slope and intercept?

    Hint

    Take the log of both sides and use the rule for the log of a product.

Practice

  1. practice Fit \(b\) to the catch records with NumPy's polyfit on the logs.
